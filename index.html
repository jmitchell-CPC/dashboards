Headline
Bookings are holding level with last year: 42 confirmed (vs 43, −2.3%) from 15.0% fewer leads — 216 vs 254 — lifting conversion to 19.4% (vs 16.9%) through October.
Confirmed guests are up +9.2% to 1,012 (vs 927), and average party size rose to 31.0 (vs 29.4). Open Prospect is roughly level (80 vs 84), but both years carry leads that were never closed out in Tripleseat: 66 of this year’s 80 were submitted January–June, and 71 of last year’s 84 were submitted August–October. Booked and conversion are the most reliable apples-to-apples signals here — read Lost/Prospect directionally.
October vs. September: leads eased to 12 from 15 (−20.0%), while bookings held at 5 vs 6 and conversion edged up to 41.7% from 40.0% (+1.7 pp). October’s closes were Stellar Health (44 guests, Oct 21), Belardi Wong (30, Dec 9), Akerman LLP (15, Nov 10), a 35-guest event on Dec 5, and a 25-guest event on Nov 3 that came in by email. Two September leads also closed after last month’s report (a 40-guest event on Nov 6 and a 20-guest event on Oct 22), so September now reads 6 of 15 (40.0%), up from 4 of 14. Five October leads are still open — including two large parties, 1823 Partners (100 guests) and Huntington / Capstone Partners (65) — so the month’s conversion can still move.
Watch: the lead total hides a split. January–June ran +25% ahead of 2025 (165 vs 132 leads), but July–October came in at 51 vs 122 — less than half of last year’s volume. October alone was 12 vs 45, and October 2025 was last year’s busiest month (10 bookings), so it is a tough comparison; still, 2026 has been at or under 18 leads a month since June. Conversion has offset most of it — bookings were 12% ahead through September (37 vs 33) and are now 2.3% behind — but the top of the funnel is what to rebuild for the holiday season. Also note that Tripleseat is not recording a reason or date for lost leads (those fields are blank on all 94 lost this year), so we can see how many leads are lost but not why.

Conversion is ahead — and holding
Conversion was choppy early in the year on both sides — swinging 5%–22% month to month — but 2026 broke ahead in June (33.3%) and July (57.1%) and has stayed ahead: 17.6% in August (vs 16.7%), 40.0% in September (vs 17.9%) and 41.7% in October (vs 22.2%). That makes five straight months above 2025 and a year-to-date rate of 19.4% vs 16.9%.
Monthly samples are small (7–18 leads a month since June), and open leads remain from September and October — so single-month swings like July’s 57.1% on 7 leads should be read as directional. The multi-month view (July–October: 35.3% vs 19.7%) is the steadier signal.
Party Size Distribution — Current vs Prior
How large are guest parties?
The 41–60 guest band grew +45.0% YTD (20 → 29) while the 16–25 band contracted −27.7% (94 → 68) and 26–40 fell −26.2% (65 → 48). The smallest parties held (1–15: 54 → 52) and 61+ was nearly flat (21 → 19). The shift toward larger parties is why average party size rose to 31.0 vs 29.4, and why confirmed guests are up 9.2% even with fewer leads and one fewer booking.
