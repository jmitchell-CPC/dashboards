Headline
Bookings have pulled ahead of last year: 35 confirmed (vs 33, +6.1%) from slightly fewer leads — 203 vs 209 (−2.9%) — lifting conversion to 17.2% (vs 15.8%) through September.
Confirmed guests are up +7.2% to 803 (vs 749), and average party size held steady at 30.4 (vs 29.9). Open Prospect looks much larger this year (76 vs 50), but that’s mostly a timing effect: this year’s leads are still working through the funnel, while last year’s have had a full year to resolve to Won or Lost. Booked and conversion are the most reliable apples-to-apples signals here — read Lost/Prospect directionally.
September vs. August: leads cooled to 14 from 17 (−17.6%), but bookings rose to 4 from 3 and conversion jumped to 28.6% from 17.6% (+11.0 pp). The month’s closes were John F Dillon (7 guests), Bank of America (15 guests, confirmed five days after submitting), and two meaningful Q4 events — Sojitz Corporation of America (70 guests, Oct 27) and TikTok (44 guests, Dec 9). Five September leads are still open (Brook Green USA, Dechert LLP, LeMaitre, Schrodinger and one unnamed lead), so the month’s conversion can still move.
Watch: the flat-looking lead total hides a split. January–June ran +25% ahead of 2025 (165 vs 132 leads), but July–September came in at 38 vs 77 — about half of last year’s volume — and September alone was 14 vs 28. Conversion has more than offset it so far, but the top of the funnel is the thing to rebuild heading into Q4. Also note that Tripleseat is not recording a reason or date for lost leads (those fields are blank on all 92 lost this year), so we can see how many leads are lost but not why.

Conversion is ahead — and holding
Conversion was choppy early in the year on both sides — swinging 5%–22% month to month — but 2026 broke ahead in June (33.3%) and July (57.1%) and has stayed ahead: 17.6% in August (vs 16.7%) and 28.6% in September (vs 17.9%). That makes four straight months above 2025 and a year-to-date rate of 17.2% vs 15.8%.
Monthly samples are small (7–17 leads a month since June), and five September leads are still open — so single-month swings like July’s 57.1% on 7 leads should be read as directional. The quarterly view (Q3: 28.9% vs 18.2%) is the steadier signal.
Party Size Distribution — Current vs Prior
How large are guest parties?
The 41–60 guest band grew +73.3% YTD (15 → 26) while the 26–40 band contracted −24.1% (58 → 44) and 16–25 eased to 66 from 72. Smaller parties (1–15) picked up (45 → 50). The shift toward larger parties is why average party size holds at 30.4 vs 29.9, and why confirmed guests are up even though leads are not.
